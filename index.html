Hybrid acceptance | best of both

Open-loop everywhere

(chicken-and-egg of acceptance)
BandZahlen
Plain words for the two models. Open-loop = the band rides the normal Visa/Mastercard network, so it works at any shop's terminal from day one — but we only see the merchant + amount, not the items. Closed-loop = our own payment terminal, which only works where we've installed it (the school canteen) — there we see the actual items bought. Cold-start = the chicken-and-egg problem: parents won't buy if few shops accept it, and shops won't install our terminal if few kids carry the band; we beat it by concentrating at schools where kid density is high. Hybrid = open-loop everywhere for usability + our terminal at schools for the data.

BandZahlen(Reg (EU) 2015/751; gohenry & Greenlight, n.d.)
Say the hardware base price out loud once while walking the stack: €19.99. Interchange = the small fee a merchant's bank pays the card issuer (us) on every card payment — but the EU caps it at ~0.2%, so on a €2.40 snack it's about half a cent; it's a rounding error, not the model. Subscription is the engine — gohenry/Greenlight deliberately chose subscription over interchange so they don't need kids to spend more. Hardware = modest one-time margin, priced to protect quality perception. School fees stays the smallest bar — schools' real value is density + item-level data; the merchant fee is incidental. Pre-empt "what do schools actually pay for?" without overselling it.

This is the honest-trade-off move he rewards. Razor-and-Blade = selling the device cheap/at a loss to make money on what comes after (like printers + ink) — we deliberately don't, to protect quality perception. And we could monetise children's data — we won't, because trust is the entire value.
Section 6 · Honest Trade-offs
Honest trade-offs · the model

Freemium bleed

Free payments still cost us
EU interchange ≈ 0.2%

The free tier gets the band on the wrist, but every tap triggers processing cost. Interchange is capped at ~0.2%, so spend alone won't subsidise free users.

Regulatory dependency

We ride a partner's licence, we're not a bank.

By law we can't hold customer money ourselves. The model depends on a licensed BaaS/EMI partner for wallets and compliance, a real dependency, not a formality.

Cold-start

The school-terminal side is sales-heavy to build.

Open-loop works day one at any terminal, but item-level data needs our own POS at schools. Winning merchants is a grind, not a flip of a switch. How we beat it: seed terminals free at high-subscriber-density schools, parent demand pulls the school in.

BandZahlen(Reg (EU) 2015/751)
One honest line each, calm not alarmist. Model-side trade-offs: freemium bleed, regulatory dependency, cold-start — and how we beat the last one.
Section 6 · Honest Trade-offs
Honest trade-offs · trust & data

Privacy vs surveillance

Location + heart-rate on a child walks a fine line.

Parents want visibility; kids deserve dignity. We square it by never monetising children's data, trust is the product, not the dataset.

Item-level limits

Full purchase detail only at partner venues.

At our school terminals we see what was bought. Everywhere else it's merchant + category, honest about where the data moat is thin.

Owning these trade-offs is what makes the model credible and pre-empts the hard questions.
